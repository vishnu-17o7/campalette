# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Designers, artists, developers, and photographers — people who work with color. They encounter colors in the real world and want to capture, study, and reuse them in their work.

## Product Purpose

Campalette solves two core jobs:
1. **Capture-to-palette**: Photograph a scene (sunset, fabric, architecture) and extract a color palette for use in a future project.
2. **Pick-to-match**: Tap a single color (a shirt, a wall, a product) and get a harmonious palette that complements it.

Success means the app feels like a natural extension of the creative process — quick to capture, satisfying to explore, and reliable to save from.

## Brand Personality

Comforting, smooth, tactically precise. The app should feel like a well-made tool in the hands of someone who cares about craft — warm but not cute, refined but not sterile, precise but not clinical.

## Anti-references

- Generic Material Design apps with default blue/red palettes and no personality
- Overly playful or childish color tools with chunky icons and bright primary colors everywhere
- Clinical, sterile interfaces that feel like enterprise software
- Apps that prioritize showing off the camera over the color extraction experience

## Design Principles

1. **Color is the hero** — Every screen should let the colors breathe. The interface steps back so the captured palette is the focal point.
2. **Tactile precision** — Interactions feel deliberate and satisfying. Haptics, animations, and transitions communicate that the tool is crafted, not assembled.
3. **Warm editorial craft** — The visual language draws from artist studios and editorial design: serif headlines, generous whitespace, paper-like surfaces. Not cold or digital-feeling.
4. **Quick to capture, deep to explore** — The path from "I see a color" to "I have a saved palette" should be fast. Exploration and editing should reward curiosity.
5. **Respect the creative workflow** — A tool for people who make things. Professional without being intimidating, beautiful without being distracting.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard Material 3 accessibility. Support for reduced motion, proper content descriptions for screen readers, and sufficient contrast ratios on interactive elements.
